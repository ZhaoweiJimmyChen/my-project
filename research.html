Research and Publications

Publications

  1. Abstract summary: This study evaluates how well archival research findings travel across time periods and geographic settings. By reproducing and extending a large set of strategic management results, the paper helps clarify when empirical findings are robust enough to support broader theory building.

    Official paper page

  2. Abstract summary: This review revisits empirical research on corruption in international business and argues for sharper theory, cleaner measurement, and stronger alignment between corruption concepts and research designs. It highlights how firm-level mechanisms and different forms of corruption matter for multinational strategy.

    Official paper page

  3. Abstract summary: This chapter connects international business research on corruption with ideas from political economy. It reviews how scholarship explains corruption's antecedents, types, and consequences, then points toward more nuanced ways to understand how multinational enterprises and policy instruments operate in corrupt environments.

    Official chapter page

  4. Abstract summary: This article develops the idea of ownership discrimination to explain why privately owned enterprises in transition economies may pursue outward foreign direct investment. Using evidence from China, it shows how perceived domestic disadvantages can motivate international expansion, while political ties can weaken that relationship.

    Official paper page
